## The Business Opportunity in One Paragraph

Approximately 83,000 Americans are diagnosed with bladder cancer each year, making it the sixth most common cancer in the US. Of these, roughly 23,000 patients present with or progress to locally advanced or metastatic disease where systemic treatment is required. Until 2024, the standard of care for these patients was platinum-based chemotherapy with median overall survival rates that had not meaningfully improved in over 30 years. The EV-302/KEYNOTE-A39 trial changed that. Padcev plus pembrolizumab is now the only regimen to demonstrate a halving of the mortality risk compared to chemotherapy in this population, across all comers regardless of PD-L1 status or cisplatin eligibility. That is the commercial opportunity.

### The Patient Profile

Understanding who gets bladder cancer is essential for understanding how to reach them and their caregivers.

**Demographics:** Bladder cancer predominantly affects older adults, with a median diagnosis age of 73. It is approximately three to four times more common in men than women. White men have the highest incidence rates. The age and gender profile shapes everything from how patients engage with digital health information to the caregiver dynamics that influence treatment decisions.

**Risk factors:** The strongest risk factor is smoking history, present in approximately 50% of cases. Occupational exposures to aromatic amines, cyclophosphamide exposure, chronic UTI, and Schistosoma haematobium infection in endemic regions are also associated.

**Histology:** Approximately 90% of bladder cancers in the US are urothelial carcinoma (transitional cell carcinoma). The remaining cases include squamous cell carcinoma, adenocarcinoma, and other rare subtypes. Padcev is indicated for urothelial carcinoma specifically, and the Nectin-4 target it uses is expressed across urothelial tumors regardless of anatomic location (bladder, renal pelvis, ureter, urethra).

**Cisplatin eligibility:** This is the most commercially critical segmentation variable. Cisplatin is the backbone of standard platinum-based chemotherapy, but approximately 50% of la/mUC patients cannot receive cisplatin because of reduced kidney function (creatinine clearance below 60 mL/min), peripheral neuropathy, poor performance status, or hearing loss. This "cisplatin-ineligible" population had no approved standard of care with proven survival benefit before Padcev plus pembrolizumab. The launch messaging must address this population specifically.

## The Treatment Landscape Before April 2024

### Historical Standard of Care

For decades, the standard first-line treatment for la/mUC was platinum-based combination chemotherapy:

**Cisplatin-eligible patients:** Gemcitabine plus cisplatin (GC) or MVAC (methotrexate, vinblastine, doxorubicin, cisplatin) with accelerated MVAC as a more tolerable alternative. Median OS with GC in landmark trials: approximately 14 months.

**Cisplatin-ineligible patients:** Gemcitabine plus carboplatin (GemCarbo) had been the most common regimen. Median OS: approximately 9 to 10 months.

The 30-year stagnation in bladder cancer outcomes is not hyperbole. Multiple phase 3 trials failed to improve on platinum-based chemotherapy in the first-line metastatic setting until the IO era.

### The Immunotherapy Era (2017 to 2024)

Starting in 2016 and accelerating through 2020, PD-1 and PD-L1 checkpoint inhibitors gained approvals in bladder cancer:

**Keytruda (pembrolizumab, Merck):** Approved in 2017 for 2L+ after platinum failure. In 2020, received accelerated approval for 1L cisplatin-ineligible patients with CPS (combined positive score) 10 or higher. This was significant but limited to a specific biomarker-selected population.

**Tecentriq (atezolizumab, Genentech/Roche):** Received accelerated approval in 2016 for 2L+ and in 2017 for 1L PD-L1-selected cisplatin-ineligible patients. This approval was subsequently voluntarily withdrawn after the IMvigor130 trial failed to meet its primary OS endpoint in unselected patients.

**Opdivo (nivolumab, Bristol Myers Squibb):** Approved for 2L+ after platinum-based chemotherapy.

**The IO plateau:** While IO therapies improved outcomes in PD-L1-high populations, they failed to improve survival compared to chemotherapy in unselected patients in the first-line setting. The field needed something more than IO monotherapy.

### The ADC Revolution: Setting the Stage for Padcev

Padcev (enfortumab vedotin) was first approved in 2019 for patients who had previously received platinum-based therapy and PD-1 or PD-L1 inhibitors. In this heavily pretreated population, it demonstrated remarkable single-agent activity. The EV-301 trial showed OS of 12.9 months versus 8.9 months for chemotherapy in 3L+ disease.

The hypothesis that combining Padcev's direct cytotoxic killing of Nectin-4-expressing urothelial cancer cells with Keytruda's immune checkpoint release could be synergistic was validated in the EV-103 basket study, which showed ORR of 73.3% in first-line cisplatin-ineligible patients treated with EV plus Keytruda. That signal led directly to EV-302.

## The Science Behind the Opportunity: ADCs Explained for PMM

Every Pfizer oncology PMM needs to be able to explain how Padcev works at three levels: to an HCP audience, to a payer audience, and in accessible language for patient education.

### What is an Antibody-Drug Conjugate?

An antibody-drug conjugate is a targeted cancer therapy that combines three components:

**1. A monoclonal antibody** that recognizes and binds to a specific target protein on the surface of cancer cells. In Padcev's case, the antibody targets Nectin-4, a cell adhesion molecule that is highly expressed on urothelial carcinoma cells and low on most normal tissues.

**2. A cytotoxic payload** that kills cells when it enters them. Padcev's payload is monomethyl auristatin E (MMAE), a potent microtubule disrupting agent. MMAE is too toxic to administer systemically on its own, which is why attaching it to a targeted antibody is the enabling technology.

**3. A chemical linker** that connects the antibody and the payload and is designed to be stable in circulation but cleave inside tumor cells or in the tumor microenvironment after the antibody binds and the complex is internalized.

### The Mechanism of Action in Plain Language

When enfortumab (the antibody component of Padcev) enters the bloodstream, it circulates until it finds cells expressing Nectin-4. Urothelial cancer cells express Nectin-4 at high levels. The antibody binds to Nectin-4 on the cancer cell surface. The entire ADC complex is then pulled inside the cancer cell through a process called receptor-mediated endocytosis. Inside the cell, the linker is cleaved, releasing the MMAE payload directly where it can kill the cancer cell by disrupting its microtubule network and preventing cell division.

Additionally, MMAE that is released into the tumor microenvironment can diffuse into neighboring cancer cells even if those cells do not express Nectin-4 themselves. This "bystander effect" is clinically important because bladder cancers are heterogeneous, meaning not every cell in the tumor expresses Nectin-4 at the same level.

### Why ADC Plus IO Is a Rational Combination

Padcev's MMAE-driven tumor cell killing is hypothesized to cause immunogenic cell death, meaning it releases tumor antigens in a way that activates the immune system. Pembrolizumab (Keytruda), by blocking PD-1, prevents cancer cells from suppressing the T-cell immune response. The two mechanisms are thought to work synergistically: Padcev debulks the tumor and creates immunogenic signals; Keytruda amplifies and sustains the immune response those signals generate. The clinical data from EV-302 is consistent with this mechanistic hypothesis.

## The EV-302/KEYNOTE-A39 Trial: The Clinical Foundation

This trial is the commercial foundation of everything. Every PMM at Pfizer needs to know these numbers.

**Study design:** Open-label, randomized phase 3 trial comparing enfortumab vedotin plus pembrolizumab (EV+P) versus platinum-based chemotherapy in 886 patients with previously untreated locally advanced or metastatic urothelial carcinoma.

**Patient population:** Adults with la/mUC regardless of cisplatin eligibility, PD-L1 status, or histologic subtype, with no prior systemic therapy for unresectable or metastatic disease.

**Key efficacy results:**

| Endpoint | EV + Pembrolizumab | Chemotherapy | Hazard Ratio (HR) | p-value |
|----------|-------------------|--------------|-------------------|---------|
| Median Overall Survival (OS) | 31.5 months | 16.1 months | 0.47 | Less than 0.00001 |
| Median Progression-Free Survival (PFS) | 12.5 months | 6.3 months | 0.45 | Less than 0.00001 |
| Objective Response Rate (ORR) | 67.7% | 44.4% | N/A | Less than 0.0001 |
| Complete Response Rate | 29.1% | 12.5% | N/A | Less than 0.0001 |

**Subgroup analysis highlights:** The survival benefit was consistent across all prespecified subgroups, including cisplatin-eligible patients, cisplatin-ineligible patients, patients with PD-L1 CPS below 10, patients with liver metastases, and patients with upper tract disease.

**Safety:** Common adverse events included peripheral sensory neuropathy (52%), alopecia (50%), fatigue (47%), maculopapular rash (42%), decreased appetite (41%), pruritus (38%), and hyperglycemia (28%). Grade 3 or higher treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 55.9% of patients receiving EV plus pembrolizumab versus 69.5% for chemotherapy. The safety profile is manageable with dose modifications and proactive adverse event monitoring.

**The 30-complete-response data point:** The 29.1% complete response rate with EV plus pembrolizumab deserves special emphasis in HCP messaging. Complete response means no detectable tumor on imaging. In a disease that historically achieved complete responses in approximately 5 to 8% of first-line patients with chemotherapy, a 29% complete response rate is clinically transformative. Some complete responders have ongoing responses extending beyond two years in the follow-up data.

## Market Trends and Tailwinds

**ADC platform proliferation:** As of 2024, there are more than 100 ADCs in clinical development globally. The ADC concept has been validated by Kadcyla, Enhertu, Trodelvy, and now Padcev. Oncologists, payers, and patients are increasingly familiar with the ADC class and comfortable with its risk-benefit profile.

**PD-L1-agnostic treatment options:** One of the most commercially important aspects of EV-302 is that the benefit extended to patients with PD-L1 CPS below 10. Prior to this approval, the only approved 1L option for cisplatin-ineligible patients without high PD-L1 was carboplatin-based chemotherapy. This creates a clear and specific population for which Padcev is the only proven effective option.

**Biomarker simplicity:** Unlike several oncology products where prescribing requires companion diagnostic testing (PD-L1 testing, FGFR mutation testing, HER2 testing), Padcev's benefit is not dependent on a biomarker test. Nectin-4 is expressed in the vast majority of urothelial carcinomas. This reduces the barrier to prescribing in community oncology settings where access to rapid biomarker testing can be a practical constraint.

**Academic-to-community diffusion:** Bladder cancer treatment patterns begin at academic medical centers and specialized urothelial cancer programs, then diffuse to community oncology practices over a 12 to 24-month window after a major label change. The launch strategy must sequence investments to capitalize on this diffusion pattern.
