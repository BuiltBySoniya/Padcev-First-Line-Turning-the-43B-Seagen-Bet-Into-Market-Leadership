# Patient Journey Map: The Bladder Cancer Experience from Symptom to System

**Deliverable 05 of 07**
**Project:** Padcev First-Line Launch Strategy
**Purpose:** Map the complete patient journey through bladder cancer diagnosis, staging, treatment initiation, and ongoing management to identify where Pfizer can improve patient access, reduce treatment delays, and support patients and caregivers throughout the Padcev experience

---

## Why Patient Journey Mapping Matters for a PMM

A patient journey map is not a clinical pathway document. It is a commercial strategy tool. It reveals where patients fall through the cracks before they reach the right treatment, which caregivers and advocates influence the treatment decision, where fear and misinformation create barriers, and where Pfizer's marketing investment will have the greatest impact on both patient outcomes and commercial results.

For Padcev, the patient journey is particularly important because bladder cancer is a relatively underpublicized disease with a historically weak patient advocacy infrastructure compared to breast cancer or lung cancer. Many patients receive a bladder cancer diagnosis without knowing that the treatment landscape has changed dramatically in 2024.

## Stage 1: The Pre-Diagnosis Experience

### What Happens Before the Oncologist Visit

Bladder cancer has a characteristic symptom that most patients ignore for longer than is clinically ideal: hematuria, or blood in the urine. Visible hematuria is often intermittent and painless in the early stages, which leads patients to delay seeking evaluation. Population studies suggest the average time between first hematuria symptom and urologist visit is approximately 3 to 6 months.

**Who the patient sees first:** In most cases, a primary care physician (PCP) or urgent care provider evaluates the initial complaint. The PCP typically orders a urinalysis and refers to urology if blood is confirmed.

**Commercial implication:** Pfizer has no promotional role at this stage. But patient advocacy programs and disease awareness initiatives funded through medical education grants can support earlier hematuria evaluation campaigns. The Bladder Cancer Advocacy Network (BCAN) runs awareness programs that Pfizer can support through unrestricted educational grants.

### The Urology Visit

The urologist performs cystoscopy, the primary diagnostic procedure. If a tumor is visualized, TURBT (transurethral resection of bladder tumor) is performed for biopsy and staging. The urologist determines whether the cancer is non-muscle-invasive (typically managed with intravesical BCG), muscle-invasive (typically staged further for cystectomy vs. trimodality therapy), or already locally advanced or metastatic at presentation.

**The referral moment:** When muscle-invasive or advanced disease is confirmed, the urologist refers to a medical oncologist or urologic oncologist for systemic treatment planning. This referral can take days at a high-volume academic center or weeks in a community setting where oncology access is limited.

**Commercial implication:** Reducing the time between urology diagnosis and oncology referral improves time-to-treatment and outcomes. Pfizer can support the urology-to-oncology care transition through educational tools that help urologists understand when to refer urgently and how to communicate the diagnosis to oncology colleagues with the clinical context needed to expedite the first oncology appointment.

## Stage 2: The Oncology Entry Point

### The First Oncology Appointment

For a patient newly referred for la/mUC, the first oncology visit involves several parallel activities:

**Clinical staging confirmation:** CT scan, possibly PET scan, bone scan if bone metastases suspected. Pathology review to confirm urothelial histology. Performance status assessment (ECOG PS).

**Laboratory evaluation:** Comprehensive metabolic panel, CBC, creatinine clearance (to determine cisplatin eligibility), hepatic function.

**No mandatory biomarker testing:** Unlike lung cancer (EGFR, ALK, PD-L1) or breast cancer (HER2, hormone receptor, BRCA), la/mUC treatment with EV plus pembrolizumab does not require companion diagnostic testing before initiating Padcev. This is a significant simplification of the treatment pathway.

**The cisplatin eligibility conversation:** The oncologist calculates creatinine clearance (CrCl). CrCl above 60 mL/min is generally considered cisplatin-eligible, though other factors (neuropathy, performance status) also influence this determination. This conversation should happen at the first oncology visit, and it immediately creates two clinical pathways.

**The patient's emotional state:** Research on cancer patient decision-making consistently shows that patients in the first oncology appointment after a new cancer diagnosis are processing significant psychological distress. Information absorption is impaired, and patients rarely retain more than 30 to 40% of what they are told in this appointment. This has direct implications for patient education material design: materials must be extremely clear, simple, and reinforced across multiple touchpoints.

### What Patients and Caregivers Do Between Appointments

Before the second oncology appointment or treatment initiation, approximately 70% of cancer patients report using the internet to research their diagnosis and treatment options. For bladder cancer specifically:

**Primary information sources:**
- Cancer.gov and American Cancer Society website (trusted, comprehensive)
- Bladder Cancer Advocacy Network website (BCAN.org)
- PubMed abstracts (rare but increasing among educated patients)
- Patient forums and communities (MyBladderCancerTeam, Facebook groups)
- Pfizer's branded patient website (for patients referred there by their oncologist)

**What patients are searching for:**
- "Bladder cancer stage 4 life expectancy"
- "Best treatment for bladder cancer that has spread"
- "Padcev side effects"
- "Is Padcev chemotherapy"

**Commercial implication:** The Pfizer digital marketing team must ensure that branded search advertising, branded patient websites, and unbranded disease awareness content are optimized for the search queries that newly diagnosed la/mUC patients use. This is a critical digital touchpoint that many pharma teams underfund relative to its commercial importance.

## Stage 3: Treatment Decision and Initiation

### The Treatment Decision Conversation

The treatment decision conversation typically happens in the second or third oncology visit. The oncologist presents treatment options. For a cisplatin-ineligible patient in 2024, this conversation should clearly include EV plus pembrolizumab as the NCCN Category 1 preferred regimen.

**What the oncologist says (ideal state post-launch):** "Your kidney function is below the threshold for cisplatin. In the past, that meant fewer options. Now there is a combination called Padcev plus Keytruda that in a large clinical trial more than doubled median survival compared to standard chemotherapy. I recommend this combination as the first line of treatment."

**What the patient hears:** Variable. Patients with good health literacy who have done research may have already heard of Padcev. Others hear "two drugs instead of one" and worry about toxicity. The oncologist's confidence and clarity in presenting the recommendation is the single most important factor in patient willingness to start the regimen.

**The caregiver role:** For older bladder cancer patients (median age 73), a spouse, adult child, or other caregiver is often present at treatment decision appointments. Caregiver concerns about administering care during treatment, managing neuropathy or skin reactions at home, and the financial impact of the regimen can all influence whether a patient starts treatment as recommended.

**Commercial implication:** Pfizer's patient support programs should include caregiver-specific resources, not just patient-specific materials. Caregivers who feel supported and informed are more likely to encourage treatment initiation and adherence.

### The Insurance and Access Barrier

Before treatment can begin, most patients face an insurance coverage determination process:

**Step 1:** Oncologist's office submits a prior authorization request to the patient's health plan.

**Step 2:** Health plan may require documentation of clinical criteria (confirmed la/mUC diagnosis, NCCN guideline reference, documentation of cisplatin eligibility status).

**Step 3:** If approved, specialty pharmacy dispenses the Padcev component and the infusion pharmacy/hospital orders Keytruda.

**Step 4:** If denied on first submission, the oncologist's office appeals. Multiple appeals may be needed before coverage is granted.

This process can take anywhere from 3 days to 3 weeks. Every week of delay in a newly diagnosed la/mUC patient represents disease progression in a potentially rapidly progressing cancer.

**The Pfizer access intervention:** The Pfizer Oncology Together patient support program should include a Prior Authorization Support Service that proactively contacts the oncologist's office to offer PA submission assistance, standard appeal letter templates, and peer-to-peer medical director review requests. The goal is to reduce the time from prescribing decision to treatment start to less than 5 days for commercially insured patients and less than 10 days for Medicare patients.

## Stage 4: On-Treatment Experience

### The Administration Experience

Padcev is administered by intravenous infusion. The administration schedule in combination with pembrolizumab involves:

**Day 1 and Day 8 of each 3-week cycle:** Padcev infusion (typically 30 to 60 minutes per infusion)
**Day 1 of each 3-week cycle:** Pembrolizumab infusion (30 minutes)

This schedule requires patients to come to an infusion center twice in the first two weeks of each cycle. For older patients, transportation is a practical barrier. For patients who are still working, two infusion center visits per cycle over a potentially multi-year treatment course represents a significant life disruption.

**The burden of the schedule:** Patient experience research in ADC recipients consistently identifies the treatment schedule burden as one of the top barriers to treatment completion. Pfizer's patient support programming should include:
- Transportation assistance partnerships (Lyft Health, American Cancer Society Road to Recovery)
- Digital appointment reminders and pre-infusion preparation guidance
- Caregiver scheduling tools

### Managing the Key Adverse Events

**Peripheral sensory neuropathy (52% of patients):** This is the most common ADC-related adverse event and the most frequent reason for dose modification or discontinuation. The neuropathy typically begins in the hands and feet and is managed through dose delays and reductions. Patient education should set expectations about neuropathy early and encourage patients to report any tingling, numbness, or pain immediately rather than waiting until the next scheduled appointment.

**Skin reactions (maculopapular rash, 42%):** Patients should be advised to use fragrance-free moisturizers, avoid hot showers, and contact the oncology team immediately if a rash develops above Grade 1.

**Hyperglycemia (28%, particularly in patients with diabetes):** Blood glucose monitoring is important, especially in the first two cycles. Patients with pre-existing diabetes may require adjustment to their diabetes medications.

**The patient education toolkit:** Pfizer should provide nurses and patients with a "Padcev at Home" guide covering what to expect in the first 30 days, how to manage common adverse events, when to call the oncology team, and what constitutes a medical emergency requiring immediate care.

## Stage 5: Long-Term Experience and Disease Monitoring

### For Responders: Managing a New Normal

Patients who achieve response on EV plus Keytruda may remain on treatment for extended periods. The EV-302 protocol allowed treatment until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Some patients continue treatment for 24 months or more.

The long-term patient experience involves routine imaging every two to three cycles, ongoing toxicity monitoring, and the psychological experience of living with metastatic cancer in a state of disease control. Pfizer's patient engagement programs should acknowledge this experience, not just the treatment initiation phase.

**Commercial implication for adherence:** Patient who feel supported and who understand the long-term benefit of treatment are more likely to remain on treatment through manageable adverse events rather than discontinuing prematurely. Every patient who discontinues early represents both a clinical cost and a commercial cost.

### For Progressive Disease: The Transition to Second Line

Patients who progress on EV plus Keytruda will move to subsequent lines of therapy. The scientific community is actively studying what the optimal second-line regimen is for patients who have progressed on EV plus Keytruda. This is an evolving area that Medical Affairs and PMM should track for future indication opportunities.
