## The Competitive Situation in One Sentence

Pfizer is launching a product that is scientifically superior to everything in its space, partnering with its most important competitor, in a disease that has historically been underfunded for commercial investment, against a payer environment that will resist the price premium until it is forced by clinical evidence to accept it.

That is the honest version of the competitive situation. The playbook must be built on that honesty.

## Deep Dive: Keytruda (Pembrolizumab, Merck) — The Partner-Competitor

### What Makes This Relationship Commercially Unique

Keytruda is simultaneously Padcev's combination partner and its most important commercial competitor. Understanding this relationship is the most important competitive task for a Pfizer PMM.

When Padcev is administered with Keytruda, both Pfizer (Astellas) and Merck capture revenue. But in the prescribing relationship, the oncologist may be primarily affiliated with Merck's Keytruda brand. The commercial question is: when an oncologist thinks about EV plus pembrolizumab, do they think "Padcev with Keytruda" or "Keytruda with Padcev"? That framing determines long-term brand equity.

### Keytruda's Current Position in Bladder Cancer

**First-line approvals:**
- Cisplatin-ineligible patients with PD-L1 CPS 10 or higher: Keytruda monotherapy (approved 2020, accelerated approval converted to regular approval based on KEYNOTE-052 follow-up data)
- In combination with Padcev: All-comers 1L la/mUC (EV-302, approved April 2024)

**Keytruda's commercial advantages:**
- Merck has a highly experienced, large oncology sales force with deep established relationships across academic and community oncology
- Keytruda is already familiar to every oncologist. The leap from "Keytruda with chemo" to "Keytruda with Padcev" feels smaller than learning an entirely new ADC
- Merck has extensive patient support infrastructure (Merck Access Program) that oncologists' offices already use
- Keytruda's PD-L1 CPS testing infrastructure is already in place at most oncology practices

**Where Keytruda monotherapy falls short:**
- The KEYNOTE-052 trial showed Keytruda monotherapy ORR of 29% in 1L unselected patients, versus 67.7% for EV plus Keytruda in EV-302
- Median OS for Keytruda monotherapy in the full KEYNOTE-052 population was approximately 11.3 months in longer follow-up
- Even in PD-L1 CPS 10+ patients, EV-302 data shows EV plus Keytruda is superior to Keytruda alone
- Keytruda monotherapy leaves cisplatin-ineligible, PD-L1-low patients with no better option than carboplatin-based chemotherapy

**The strategic Pfizer position:** Pfizer cannot attack Keytruda directly in its promotional materials because Keytruda is a combination partner. Instead, Pfizer must implicitly demonstrate that the EV plus pembrolizumab combination represents a meaningfully superior approach compared to any Keytruda-containing monotherapy regimen. The statistical comparison of EV-302 versus KEYNOTE-052 historical data, while not a head-to-head randomized comparison, is a credible basis for clinical conversations.

**What to say to an oncologist who defaults to Keytruda monotherapy for PD-L1 high cisplatin-ineligible patients:** "The EV-302 data shows that even in PD-L1 high patients, adding enfortumab vedotin to pembrolizumab improved outcomes. The combination is the new standard of care in this setting regardless of PD-L1 status." This is fair, accurate, and does not require a direct attack on Keytruda.

## Deep Dive: Platinum-Based Chemotherapy — The Inertia Competitor

### Why Gemcitabine Plus Cisplatin Is Harder to Beat Than You Think

Pfizer's most important competitive task in the first 12 months of the launch is not winning against another targeted therapy. It is overcoming the inertia of platinum-based chemotherapy as the default treatment for newly diagnosed la/mUC patients who are cisplatin-eligible.

**Why chemotherapy persists:**
- Zero drug cost to the hospital. Gemcitabine and cisplatin are generic and inexpensive. Every hospital oncology pharmacist knows how to order, mix, and administer them.
- Every oncologist learned to give GC in residency and fellowship. The mental model is already formed.
- The EV-302 survival improvement, while dramatic, will take time to change deeply ingrained prescribing habits. Oncologists who have been using GC for 20 years may want to see the data presented at multiple major meetings before changing their default approach.
- Infusion center logistics. Adding ADC administration requires new protocols, nursing training, and often a new drug on formulary.

**What changes oncologist behavior:**
- Peer-to-peer academic influence from key opinion leaders (KOLs) who treat high volumes of bladder cancer and have already adopted EV plus Keytruda
- Patient requests. As patient advocacy groups and websites update their information to reflect the new 1L standard, patients will increasingly ask about Padcev
- Practice guidelines. NCCN (National Comprehensive Cancer Network) guideline updates listing EV plus pembrolizumab as a Category 1 preferred regimen in 1L la/mUC are critical
- Medical education events featuring the EV-302 data presented by investigators and KOLs

**The cisplatin-ineligible priority:** The commercial strategy should prioritize cisplatin-ineligible patients first because the clinical argument is cleanest and the competitive dynamic is simplest. These patients had no proven survival-extending first-line option before Padcev plus Keytruda. The counterfactual is carboplatin-based chemo with median OS of 9 to 10 months. Every cisplatin-ineligible patient diagnosed with la/mUC is a case where Padcev plus Keytruda has the most unambiguous clinical case.

## Deep Dive: Nivolumab Plus Chemotherapy (CheckMate 901, BMS)

### The Emerging Threat in Cisplatin-Eligible Patients

Bristol Myers Squibb's CheckMate 901 trial demonstrated that nivolumab plus gemcitabine-cisplatin improved OS compared to gemcitabine-cisplatin alone in cisplatin-eligible patients.

**CheckMate 901 key data:**
- Median OS: 21.7 months (nivolumab plus GC) versus 18.9 months (GC alone) — HR 0.78
- FDA approval for this indication: August 2024

**Why this matters for Padcev:** BMS's approval creates an alternative regimen for cisplatin-eligible patients that oncologists can use instead of EV plus Keytruda. The head-to-head comparison is unfavorable to BMS: EV-302 showed 31.5 months median OS for EV plus Keytruda versus 16.1 months for chemotherapy. But CheckMate 901 was a different trial with a different population and chemotherapy backbone, so direct statistical comparison is not possible in promotional materials.

**The commercial response:** The Pfizer sales team must be prepared to address the BMS data with these specific points:

1. EV-302 enrolled both cisplatin-eligible and cisplatin-ineligible patients, making it a broader label. CheckMate 901 enrolled only cisplatin-eligible patients.

2. EV plus Keytruda data shows a median OS improvement of 15.4 months over the control arm (31.5 minus 16.1). The Opdivo plus GC data shows 2.8 months improvement (21.7 minus 18.9). These are different comparisons, but the magnitude of benefit is difficult to ignore.

3. NCCN guidelines classify EV plus pembrolizumab as Category 1 preferred for ALL la/mUC patients regardless of cisplatin eligibility. Opdivo plus GC is listed but not as the preferred regimen.

4. Both regimens carry significant toxicity. Comparing the specific adverse event profiles should be done with an MSL (Medical Science Liaison), not a sales representative.

## Deep Dive: Enhertu (Trastuzumab Deruxtecan, AstraZeneca/Daiichi Sankyo) — The ADC Mindshare Battle

### Why This Is an Indirect but Important Competitor

Enhertu (T-DXd) is not approved or widely used in bladder cancer. It is approved for HER2+ breast cancer, HER2+ gastric and gastroesophageal junction cancer, and HER2-ultralow or HER2-low breast cancer. But Enhertu is critically important to Padcev's commercial success for one reason: it is shaping how oncologists think about ADCs.

**The Enhertu halo effect, positive and negative:**

Positive: Enhertu has demonstrated that ADCs can achieve remarkable response rates in solid tumors, including complete responses in heavily pretreated patients. Every oncologist who has used Enhertu or read about it has been impressed by ADC efficacy data. Padcev's 67.7% ORR and 29.1% CR rate in 1L bladder cancer is consistent with the "ADC class effect" narrative that Enhertu has established.

Negative: Enhertu's main class-related toxicity is interstitial lung disease (ILD), which can be severe and fatal. Some oncologists have generalized ADC toxicity concerns based on Enhertu's ILD profile to the entire ADC class, even though each ADC has its own distinct toxicity profile. Padcev's primary toxicities are peripheral sensory neuropathy and skin reactions, not ILD. Sales representatives must be prepared to distinguish Padcev's toxicity profile from the class perception created by Enhertu.

**The competitive message:** Padcev is a member of the ADC class, but its specific toxicity profile, its specific target (Nectin-4 rather than HER2), and its specific clinical context are distinct. The ADC class narrative is a tailwind for awareness but requires careful navigation to prevent toxicity misconceptions.

## Competitive Positioning Framework

This framework guides how Pfizer positions Padcev against each competitive scenario.

### Scenario 1: Oncologist defaults to GC chemotherapy for cisplatin-eligible patient

**What to say:** "The EV-302 data showed a 47% reduction in the risk of death for patients receiving EV plus pembrolizumab compared to platinum-based chemotherapy. Median OS was 31.5 versus 16.1 months, a 15.4-month improvement. This benefit was consistent across all subgroups including cisplatin-eligible patients, PD-L1-low patients, and patients with visceral metastases. NCCN now lists EV plus pembrolizumab as the Category 1 preferred regimen."

**What not to say:** Anything that implies platinum chemotherapy is harmful or contraindicated. It remains appropriate for patients who cannot access or afford Padcev.

### Scenario 2: Oncologist uses Keytruda monotherapy for cisplatin-ineligible, PD-L1-high patient

**What to say:** "Keytruda is an important part of the EV plus pembrolizumab combination. The EV-302 data showed that even patients who are PD-L1 CPS 10 or higher benefited from adding enfortumab vedotin to pembrolizumab. The response rates and survival outcomes in the EV-302 PD-L1-high subgroup were superior to historical Keytruda monotherapy data. The combination is more effective than either component alone."

**What not to say:** Do not directly claim Padcev is superior to Keytruda in promotional materials. This requires careful MLR review.

### Scenario 3: Oncologist has concerns about neuropathy from Padcev

**What to say:** "Peripheral sensory neuropathy is the primary ADC-related adverse event of concern with enfortumab vedotin. In EV-302, it occurred in 52% of patients overall, with Grade 3 or higher in 5.8%. Dose reduction and dose delay guidelines are well-established and most patients can continue treatment with appropriate management. The product label includes specific guidance on monitoring and dose modification."

**What not to say:** Minimize the neuropathy signal. It is real, it is the most important patient safety consideration, and dismissing it damages credibility.

### Scenario 4: Payer asks why they should pay $300K+ for this combination

**What to say:** Present the health economic argument: "A patient who achieves complete response on EV plus pembrolizumab and has no subsequent lines of systemic therapy represents a significant cost reduction compared to a patient who receives multiple sequential lines of therapy over 14 to 18 months. The QALY improvement associated with 15 additional months of median survival, factored for quality of life, suggests a cost-per-QALY in the range of $140,000 to $180,000, which is within the accepted payer threshold for a serious cancer indication."
