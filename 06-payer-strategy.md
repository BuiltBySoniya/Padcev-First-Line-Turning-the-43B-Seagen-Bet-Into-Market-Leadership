## The Market Access Reality

A product without formulary access is commercially worthless regardless of how strong its clinical data is. For a combination regimen with a potential annual cost of $350,000 or more for the Padcev component alone, payer access is not a given. The Pfizer market access team must work proactively to ensure that by the time oncologists begin prescribing Padcev in the first-line setting, the path from prescription to patient is as short and frictionless as possible.

For Padcev's first-line indication specifically, the market access challenge is more manageable than it might appear. The product was already approved for 2L+ disease and has existing formulary coverage at most major health plans and hospital systems. The first-line label expansion requires updated formulary coverage criteria, not entirely new coverage determinations. This creates a shorter access timeline than a completely new product launch.

## The US Payer Landscape for First-Line la/mUC

### Payer Segments

**Commercial insurance (approximately 35 to 40% of la/mUC patients):**
Large commercial plans (UnitedHealth, Anthem/Elevance, Cigna, Aetna, Humana) use pharmacy and therapeutics (P&T) committees to make formulary decisions. These committees meet quarterly in most cases and review new clinical evidence against established coverage criteria. For Padcev's 1L expansion, P&T reviewers will examine EV-302 data against the NCCN guideline listing and comparative safety and efficacy data.

**Medicare (approximately 55 to 60% of la/mUC patients):**
Given the median patient age of 73, Medicare is the dominant payer for this population. Medicare Part B covers physician-administered drugs including both Padcev (IV infusion) and pembrolizumab (IV infusion). Part B reimbursement is based on Average Sales Price (ASP) plus 6% in most settings, or ASP plus 3.8% under sequestration. Pfizer must ensure appropriate ASP reporting and that hospital outpatient drug departments understand Part B billing for the combination.

Medicare Advantage plans (managed Medicare) introduce additional variability. Coverage determination speed and prior authorization requirements vary significantly across Medicare Advantage organizations. The field access team must map the top Medicare Advantage plans in each geography and engage them proactively.

**Medicaid (approximately 5% of la/mUC patients):**
Medicaid coverage for specialty oncology drugs varies by state and managed Medicaid plan. State fee-for-service Medicaid programs are required to cover FDA-approved drugs used for approved indications under the Medicaid rebate program. Managed Medicaid plans can impose prior authorization requirements.

## The Health Economic Argument

Payers do not make formulary decisions based on efficacy data alone. They require a health economic framework that justifies the cost relative to existing options.

### Cost-Effectiveness Analysis

**The QALY framework:**
In the US, there is no formal regulatory requirement to demonstrate cost-effectiveness. However, ICER (the Institute for Clinical and Economic Review) increasingly influences payer decision-making by publishing cost-effectiveness analyses for high-cost oncology products. Pfizer should proactively engage with ICER's assessment process for Padcev.

**The core economic argument for Padcev:**

Baseline patient trajectory (chemotherapy): A cisplatin-ineligible patient treated with carboplatin-based chemotherapy has a median OS of approximately 10 months and median PFS of approximately 5 months. Over 10 months, this patient will receive 5 to 7 cycles of carboplatin-based chemotherapy, followed by 4 to 6 months of second-line therapy (historically Keytruda or Taxol-based regimens), followed by declining performance status and hospice. Total cost of this trajectory: approximately $120,000 to $160,000 in drug and disease management costs.

EV plus pembrolizumab trajectory: Median OS of 31.5 months with median PFS of 12.5 months. A patient on EV plus pembrolizumab who achieves response may continue treatment for 12 to 18 months before progression, potentially achieving complete response with extended treatment-free intervals. For a 29.1% complete responder who achieves durable CR, the subsequent treatment cost is dramatically lower. Total cost of this trajectory at current list price: approximately $350,000 to $420,000 for the Padcev component through median PFS, plus Keytruda costs.

**The honest cost-effectiveness picture:** At list price, EV plus pembrolizumab is more expensive than chemotherapy on a per-patient basis. The cost-effectiveness argument is built on:

1. Quality-adjusted life years (QALYs): The 15.4-month OS improvement translates to approximately 1.2 to 1.4 QALYs, depending on quality of life adjustments. At a cost of $200,000 to $230,000 per QALY (using net price after rebates, not list price), this approaches the upper range of ICER's $100,000 to $150,000 per QALY benchmark but is within the range that many payers accept for a serious cancer indication.

2. Complete responder economics: The 29.1% of patients who achieve complete response and maintain durable responses represent a subpopulation with dramatically lower long-term treatment costs. Modeled over a five-year horizon, these complete responders offset some of the higher upfront drug cost with lower subsequent therapy utilization.

3. Workforce productivity (for the minority of working-age patients): Bladder cancer affects a substantial number of patients who are under 65 and still working. Productivity gains from 15 additional months of life and better functional status represent an economic benefit not captured in traditional clinical cost analyses.

### Key Messages for Payer Medical Directors

"The EV-302 OS improvement of 15.4 months is among the largest ever demonstrated in a first-line solid tumor phase 3 trial. The relevant comparator for payer decision-making is not cost versus chemotherapy but cost versus the downstream treatment expense and quality-of-life burden of a patient who fails chemotherapy and requires multiple lines of systemic therapy."

"The 29% complete response rate suggests a meaningful proportion of patients will have substantially reduced long-term treatment costs due to durable disease control."

"At net price (accounting for Pfizer's mandatory Medicaid rebate and commercial contracting discounts), the cost-per-QALY analysis for EV plus pembrolizumab falls within the range of commonly accepted oncology thresholds."

## Formulary Strategy

### Tier and Prior Authorization Approach

**Target formulary position:** Preferred Tier 4 or Specialty status on commercial formularies, with prior authorization based on clinical criteria consistent with the FDA label (confirmed la/mUC, first-line treatment-naive setting).

**Prior authorization criteria to negotiate with payers:**
- Confirmed pathology report documenting urothelial carcinoma
- Documented locally advanced or metastatic staging
- No prior systemic chemotherapy or immunotherapy for this indication
- Prescribing physician is an oncologist (not a urologist without oncology training)

**Criteria to resist:** PA criteria that require PD-L1 testing, FGFR testing, or any other biomarker testing as a requirement for Padcev coverage are clinically inappropriate (Padcev's benefit is biomarker-independent) and should be pushed back at the payer contracting level.

### The Institutional Formulary Process

Hospital and IDN P&T committees must approve Padcev for their formulary before infusion center nurses can administer it. Pfizer's national account managers should proactively submit formulary dossiers to the top 100 IDNs and health systems within 30 days of the 1L approval.

The formulary dossier should include:
- FDA approval letter and updated prescribing information
- EV-302 publication (NEJM)
- NCCN guideline excerpt listing EV plus pembrolizumab as Category 1 preferred
- Comparative effectiveness summary
- Budget impact model for the institution's estimated annual la/mUC patient volume
- Patient assistance program overview

## Patient Assistance and Financial Support Programs

Even with insurance coverage, patients often face substantial out-of-pocket costs for specialty oncology therapies. A patient who cannot afford their co-pay does not start treatment.

### Pfizer Oncology Together (Pfizer's Branded Patient Support)

**Co-pay assistance:** For commercially insured patients, Pfizer's co-pay assistance program should cover patient out-of-pocket costs up to an annual maximum that ensures patients pay a manageable monthly amount. Industry standard for high-cost oncology products is to target patient out-of-pocket at $25 to $50 per month.

**Patient navigation services:** A dedicated patient navigator team that helps patients understand their insurance coverage, navigate the PA process, and connect with additional financial assistance programs (NeedyMeds, HealthWell Foundation, Patient Advocate Foundation).

**Free drug program:** For uninsured and underinsured patients who do not qualify for other programs, Pfizer should provide Padcev at no cost through the established free drug pathway. Eligibility criteria typically include income below 400 to 500% of the federal poverty level and no adequate insurance coverage.

### Medicare Copay Considerations

Medicare Part B covers physician-administered drugs, but Medicare patients face 20% co-insurance after their deductible. For a $350,000 annual drug cost, 20% co-insurance represents $70,000 in patient out-of-pocket exposure. Most Medicare Advantage plans cap out-of-pocket costs, but traditional Medicare patients without Medigap supplemental insurance face significant exposure.

Pfizer cannot provide direct co-pay assistance to Medicare patients (anti-kickback statute restrictions), but can direct patients to independent charitable organizations that provide cost-sharing assistance to cancer patients. The HealthWell Foundation, Patient Advocate Foundation, and Cancer Care all operate independent Medicare patient assistance programs that Pfizer can support through unrestricted grants.
