**Deliverable 07 of 07**
**Project:** Padcev First-Line Launch Strategy
**Purpose:** Define the complete launch execution plan covering timing, promotional channels, budget allocation, cross-functional coordination, and key performance indicators for the Padcev 1L indication launch

---

## Launch Readiness Principle

A pharmaceutical launch is a time-compressed, cross-functional, regulated commercial event. Pfizer's launch for the Padcev 1L indication began before FDA approval was granted and will not be complete until community oncology adoption reaches a stable baseline.

The critical discipline in pharmaceutical launch planning is understanding the distinction between pre-approval activities (which are tightly regulated and cannot reference the unapproved indication) and post-approval activities (which operate within the FDA's promotional framework). Every item in this plan is categorized accordingly.

## Launch Timeline Overview

### Phase 1: Pre-Approval Readiness (12 to 0 Months Before Approval)

Activities in this phase are limited to internal preparation and unbranded disease education. No reference to the specific unapproved indication in field promotional activities.

**Key readiness milestones:**

**T-12 months:**
- Sales force training on ADC mechanism, Nectin-4 biology, and la/mUC disease state education (not indication-specific)
- Hospital formulary dossier preparation (for submission immediately post-approval)
- Digital property preparation: updated branded website content in draft, staged for post-approval activation
- Patient support program infrastructure ready for enrollment

**T-6 months:**
- MLR submission of all launch promotional materials (PI detail aid, sales aid, patient brochure, payer dossier)
- MSL team engaged in scientific exchange about EV-302 data at academic centers (MSLs can discuss published data in scientific exchange context)
- Market access team initiated payer pre-submission conversations

**T-3 months:**
- Final promotional material approvals from MLR
- Speaker bureau program designed and faculty identified
- National sales meeting training on approved launch messaging
- Specialty pharmacy distribution agreements finalized

**T-30 days:**
- "Lock and load" state: all materials ready, all teams trained, launch week playbook confirmed
- Advisory board final briefing on market readiness

---

### Phase 2: Launch Week and Month 1 (Day 0 to Day 30)

**Day 0 (FDA Approval announcement):**
- Pfizer press release distributed
- Branded website goes live with updated first-line indication content
- Sales force receives automated notification and updated sales materials
- Social media: Pfizer corporate channels share FDA approval announcement (unbranded disease community, patient advocacy)
- Field access teams activated for PA support enrollment

**Week 1 to 2:**
- Priority accounts (Segment B physicians at formulary-approved institutions) receive first field calls with updated indication detail
- Hospital formulary dossiers submitted to top 100 IDNs
- BCAN and patient advocacy partners share patient-facing materials through their channels
- Congress timing: If approval coincides with ASCO GU, ESMO, or AUA annual meetings, commercial presence at booth and satellite symposia should be timed accordingly

**Week 2 to 4:**
- First speaker bureau programs in priority geographies
- Digital advertising campaigns launch (Doceree, Epocrates, Healio)
- MLR-approved email outreach to target prescribers
- Specialty pharmacy operational alignment confirmed

---

### Phase 3: Market Penetration (Month 2 to Month 12)

**Month 2 to 3 — The Academic Center Push:**
Priority focus on Segment A and top Segment B accounts. National accounts managers complete formulary submissions. MSL scientific exchange continues at academic medical centers. Speaker bureau programs at 20 to 30 priority geographies with Segment A KOL faculty presenting EV-302 data.

**Month 4 to 6 — Community Oncology Expansion:**
Sales force activity shifts toward Segment B and C community oncologists. Digital education programs intensify. First KPI review against TRx (total prescriptions), NBRx (new-to-brand prescriptions), and formulary access metrics.

**Month 7 to 9 — Congress Season Intensity:**
If major oncology congresses (ESMO, ASCO-IO, SUO) occur in this window, invest heavily in medical education programs and post-congress sales calls. Updated follow-up data from EV-302 if available should be shared through Medical Affairs channels and incorporated into messaging.

**Month 10 to 12 — Year One Completion:**
First annual KPI assessment. Identify underperforming geographies and accounts for targeted support. Update messaging if new competitive data emerges (CheckMate 901 long-term follow-up, Dato-DXd trial readout timing).

## Promotional Channel Plan

### Personal Promotion (Field Force)

**Specialty sales representatives:** The primary driver of prescriber awareness and behavior change.

Call objectives for each sales call:
- Call 1 with new prescriber: EV-302 efficacy data overview, NCCN Category 1 listing, cisplatin-ineligible patient profile
- Call 2: Address specific objections or questions from Call 1; focus on appropriate patient identification and prescribing process
- Call 3: Reinforce with updated clinical data, speaker bureau program invitation, or peer-to-peer education program invitation
- Ongoing: Reinforce approved indication, support PA process, share new data as published

**MSLs (Medical Science Liaisons):** Medical Affairs, not Commercial. Engage with Segment A KOLs at academic centers on scientific exchange about EV-302 data, mechanism of action, and clinical practice questions. MSL conversations are non-promotional and reactive to physician-initiated requests.

**Field access managers (formerly known as reimbursement specialists):** Support PA submissions, hospital formulary approvals, and patient access program enrollment. Present at accounts where access barriers are identified.

### Non-Personal Promotion (Digital and Print)

**Physician digital advertising:**
- Doceree: Programmatic advertising targeting oncologists and urologic oncologists by specialty and geography
- Epocrates: Drug database advertising and clinical decision support integration
- Healio: Oncology-specific banner advertising and sponsored content
- NEJM and Lancet digital advertising: Reaches the high-science Segment A audience

**Branded website (padcev.com HCP section):** Full prescribing information, EV-302 clinical data summary, patient identification guide, and PA support resources.

**Unbranded disease awareness (BCAN partnership):** Support BCAN's patient education website and conference programming through unrestricted educational grants. BCAN reaches newly diagnosed bladder cancer patients and their caregivers at exactly the moment when treatment information is most sought.

**Email campaigns:** MLR-approved email sequences deployed through IQVIA or Symphony Health physician email databases. Segmented by specialty, geography, and prescribing history. Email content includes: approval announcement, clinical data summary, speaker bureau invitation, and NCCN guideline update notification.

**Journal advertising:** Full-page four-color print and digital advertising in:
- Journal of Clinical Oncology
- European Urology
- The Lancet Oncology
- Cancer

### Medical Education (Non-Promotional)

**Speaker bureau programs:** Faculty drawn from Segment A KOL pool. Programs run in restaurant and hotel settings in priority geographies. Target audience: Segment B and C physicians. Program format: 45-minute clinical presentation on la/mUC treatment advances followed by Q&A. CME-accredited programs developed through independent medical education organizations (separate from commercial promotion, compliant with PhRMA guidelines).

**Advisory boards:** Two types:
- National Advisory Board (10 to 15 Segment A KOLs): Meet quarterly to advise on messaging strategy, market dynamics, and unmet needs
- Regional Advisory Boards (6 to 8 physicians per region, 10 to 12 regions): Meet biannually to provide local market insight and peer-to-peer influence within their region

**Congress presence:**
Major oncology congresses where Padcev data should be featured:
- ASCO Annual Meeting (June)
- ESMO Annual Meeting (September)
- American Urological Association (AUA, May)
- Society of Urologic Oncology (SUO, December)
- Bladder Cancer Advocacy Network Summit (varies)

Activities at each congress: Scientific exhibit booth with interactive clinical data presentations, commercial booth for sales literature distribution, company-sponsored satellite symposia (where congress rules allow), and CME-accredited educational programming developed through independent medical education.

### Patient Engagement

**Patient website (padcev.com patient section):** FDA-required patient labeling information, plain-language explanation of how Padcev works, adverse event management information, and enrollment link for the Pfizer Oncology Together patient support program.

**BCAN partnership:** Sponsor BCAN's patient and caregiver support programs, including their "Tell Someone" patient support network and their annual conference.

**Social media:** Patient and caregiver audiences are increasingly active on platforms including Facebook, Instagram, and YouTube. Pfizer can engage these audiences through Pfizer corporate channels and through support of BCAN's social media education content.

## Cross-Functional Launch Coordination

A pharmaceutical launch requires coordination across multiple functions that do not report to the same PMM team. The PMM is responsible for the commercial strategy but must align with:

**Medical Affairs:** MSL deployment, medical information response development, advisory board management, and scientific exchange programs. Medical Affairs must move in parallel with but separate from commercial activities.

**Regulatory Affairs:** All promotional materials require regulatory review and approval through the MLR process. The PMM must build MLR review cycles into all campaign timelines.

**Market Access:** P&T dossier content, formulary strategy, payer contracting, and patient assistance program design.

**Commercial Operations:** Sales force sizing, territory design, targeting lists, CRM configuration, and call planning.

**Patient Services:** Patient assistance program operations, access support team staffing, and patient navigation.

**Communications and PR:** Corporate press release for FDA approval, executive media interviews, and corporate social media.

**Legal:** Promotional compliance review, contract review for speaker bureau and advisory boards, competitive intelligence gathering guidelines.

**International (ex-US teams):** EU and other market equivalents have different regulatory timelines. The US launch plan does not directly govern international activities, but sharing learnings and aligned messaging serves the global brand.

## What Defines Launch Success

Launch success for Padcev's first-line indication is defined by one metric above all others: within 12 months of approval, does EV plus pembrolizumab become the standard of care for first-line la/mUC patients in the United States as reflected in prescribing data, NCCN guideline listing, and oncologist survey research?

Secondary commercial success is measured by the revenue contribution to Pfizer's oncology portfolio and the demonstration that the Seagen acquisition is on track to deliver the returns promised to shareholders.

The PMM team's job is to create the conditions — the clinical credibility, the commercial access, the prescriber relationships, the patient support infrastructure — that make both of those outcomes possible.
